# Clarke 1904 Clinical Repertory Rubric & Remedy Density Audit

This document presents the structural audit of the parsed rubric-to-remedy distribution inside John Henry Clarke’s 1904 Clinical Repertory.

## Quantitative Findings

* **Total Parsed Rubrics**: 7,222
* **Total Remedy Entries**: 12,060
* **Average Remedy Count per Rubric**: 1.67
* **Zero-Remedy Rubrics**: 135 (represent parent-only symptoms serving as categories)
* **Maximum Remedy Count on a Single Rubric**: 9 remedies (e.g. general "Abdomen - Coldness")
* **Duplicate Rubric IDs**: 0 (resolved by appending path prefixes, e.g., `Clinical:Abdomen` vs `Causation:Abdomen`)

## Anomaly Investigation: 1.67 Remedy Average

The average of 1.67 remedies per rubric is extremely low compared to Kent’s Repertory (which averages over 15 remedies per rubric). The audit confirms this is **not a parser error or truncation defect**, but a design feature of the source:

1. **Self-Contained Lines**: In Clarke's layout, each rubric and its associated remedies are printed on a single physical line, separated by `. —`. There are no multi-line continuation lists.
2. **Clinical Focus**: Clarke's repertory is a clinical keynote index. It lists only the 1 to 3 remedies clinically verified to resolve that specific diagnosis, rather than listing every remedy known to affect the anatomical region.
3. **Parent Category Nodes**: The 135 zero-remedy rubrics represent nesting categories (e.g. `Abdomen`) which contain no direct remedies but have sub-rubrics (e.g. `Abdomen - Coldness in`) that map to remedies.
