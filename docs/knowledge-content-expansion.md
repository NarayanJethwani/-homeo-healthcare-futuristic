# Knowledge Content Expansion Plan

## Goal

Build a single question-first Knowledge experience that starts with a clear answer for a patient, then progressively reveals practical context, clinical detail, sources, and safety boundaries for students and clinicians. Each new or refreshed topic should be understandable without choosing an audience mode or opening multiple tabs.

## Publishing standard

Every priority topic should include:

1. A plain-language title and two-sentence introduction.
2. Five patient questions: what it is, what it can feel like, how it is usually checked, safe next steps, and when to seek urgent care.
3. A short “What happens next?” section that describes the care pathway without prescribing a diagnosis or treatment.
4. A deeper clinical layer with terminology, differential context, citations, and a named medical reviewer.
5. Three original visual cards: understand, recognise, and support; plus the existing Homeo Healthcare social card.

## Prioritisation rules

- Start with high-search, high-confusion questions and pages that connect to multiple symptoms or lab results.
- Add a new topic only after a content owner confirms scope, citations, clinical reviewer, safety boundaries, and canonical URL.
- Refresh existing content before publishing a duplicate topic. For example, “low Vitamin D” routes to the Vitamin D Deficiency page plus the Vitamin D test page.
- Use question-based search aliases such as “Why am I tired?”, “What does low vitamin D mean?”, and “When should I see a doctor for period pain?”.
- Keep claims proportionate: explain uncertainty, never imply a cure, and send urgent symptoms to appropriate care.
